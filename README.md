# gate io vs kucoin: Which One Costs Less Once Fees, Pair Classes and Your Country Are Counted

Two exchanges, one identical headline fee, and a search that keeps getting asked because the headline doesn't answer anything. Both Gate and KuCoin quote 0.10% maker and 0.10% taker on standard spot pairs at the entry tier. A trader turning over $10,000 a month on spot pays roughly $120 a year on either one before discounts. That's the number both marketing teams want you to stop at.

The bill that actually lands is decided somewhere else: which pair class your order hits, whether you're using limit or market orders, whether you hold the venue's token, and whether the platform is legally allowed to serve you at all. Those four things split these two exchanges apart far more than the 0.10% figure does.

## Spot looks like a tie, and it isn't one

KuCoin doesn't charge 0.10% on spot. It charges 0.10% on Class A pairs, 0.20% on Class B, and 0.30% on Class C at the entry tier. That tiering is published in KuCoin's own VIP fee table and confirmed by independent fee breakdowns.

Gate's published schedule at VIP 0 is 0.10% maker and 0.10% taker on spot, and it doesn't split the market into three price classes the way KuCoin does.

For someone buying majors, that distinction costs nothing. For someone hunting small caps — which is exactly why most people end up comparing these two exchanges — it's the whole argument. A 0.30% round trip on a KuCoin Class C pair is three times the rate Gate charges on the same kind of trade. Traders reading a comparison that only quotes "0.10% on both" are getting an average that applies to nobody.

## Futures are close, and Gate is ahead by roughly one coffee per month

Gate's VIP 0 perpetual schedule is 0.020% maker and 0.050% taker. KuCoin's is 0.020% maker and 0.060% taker. Both come from the exchanges' own documentation.

Run the numbers on $100,000 of monthly notional split evenly between maker and taker orders and no token discount:

- Gate: 0.035% blended, $35 a month, about $420 a year
- KuCoin: 0.040% blended, $40 a month, about $480 a year

Sixty dollars a year. That's a real difference and also not a reason on its own to move your whole book, which is a distinction most comparison pieces skip because "$60 saved" reads worse as a headline than "cheaper exchange."

Two caveats on Gate's futures figure. One independent collection pulled maker and taker rates straight from `api.gateio.ws` on 4 September 2026 and got −0.010% maker against 0.075% taker on BTCUSDT, which contradicts the published perpetual schedule. Contract-level pages and promotional rates can differ from the general table, so check the specific contract you plan to trade rather than trusting any blog's table, including this one. On KuCoin's side, the exchange removed its negative maker mechanism on 30 April 2026; spot maker fees are 0% from VIP 8 to VIP 12, with no rebate above that.

## The token discount goes to KuCoin, by eight dollars a year

Paying fees in KCS cuts KuCoin's spot rate by 20%, taking 0.10% to 0.08%. Paying in GT takes Gate's 0.10% to 0.09% — a roughly 10% cut. On the $10,000-a-month spot profile, that's $96 a year on KuCoin against $108 on Gate.

The interesting part is where the discounts stop working. In Gate's published table, the GT-paid column and the standard column are identical from VIP 10 upward, so the token stops buying a lower rate at exactly the tiers where the absolute numbers are large enough to matter. KuCoin's 20% KCS discount also has floors: it won't apply once a spot class rate falls below 0.025%, 0.050% or 0.075%.

KCS carries a second mechanic that GT doesn't: KuCoin distributes 50% of its daily spot trading fee revenue to KCS holders, calculated on balances at midnight UTC+8. What that pays in practice is worth being careful about. The widely repeated 7% to 18% annualised figures come from a period of much higher volume; independent 2026 estimates land closer to 1% to 4% for typical retail holdings, and KuCoin publishes no forward guarantee. Treat it as a rebate against trading you were doing anyway, not as a yield strategy.

## Coin counts depend entirely on who's counting

CoinGecko tracked 1,687 Gate coins across 2,205 pairs in September 2026, against 889 KuCoin coins across 1,071 pairs. An earlier August 2026 pull had Gate at 1,549 coins and KuCoin at 845. Gate's own transparency reports claim 4,500+ spot assets; KuCoin's own site claims 1,000+.

The gap holds up whichever set you use — Gate lists roughly twice as many independently tracked coins — but the absolute numbers differ by hundreds depending on the source, because self-reported listings and CoinGecko's tracked spot pairs measure different things. What actually matters when you're choosing isn't the count. It's whether the venue carries the specific token you want, and whether the order book still has depth three weeks after the listing announcement, when the airdrop farmers have gone.

## Where KuCoin is genuinely better: bots

KuCoin ships six native trading bots — spot grid, infinity grid, futures grid, DCA, margin grid and smart rebalancing — with no subscription on top of normal trading fees, and you can run several at once. Third-party platforms charge $20 to $100 a month for broadly comparable grid logic, so this isn't a small perk.

One structural wrinkle: because KuCoin's spot fee is flat rather than maker-lower at the base tier, a spot grid bot resting limit orders gets no maker advantage from doing so. The same bot on KuCoin futures does, because the futures schedule does reward resting orders. If your strategy depends on capturing the maker side on spot, KuCoin's spot schedule works against you even while its bot tooling works for you.

Gate has copy trading, APIs and automation, but its native bot line-up isn't the thing people switch to Gate for. If bot tooling is your primary requirement, this section is the one to weigh hardest.

## Where Gate is genuinely ahead: everything that isn't crypto

Gate's June 2026 transparency report, which is the venue's own accounting, lists spot volume of $66.1 billion for the month (up 50.8% month on month), a 10.8% share of derivatives open interest putting it third globally, roughly $280 million in monthly prediction market volume, and close to $20 billion in monthly ETF trading volume. Gate Stocks made 10,000-plus US tickers available at zero trading fees from 31 July 2026, per the company.

Options, stock CFDs, tokenised equities, pre-IPO allocations and a wealth management arm all sit in the same account as spot and futures. KuCoin's product set is narrower and centred on crypto plus its bot suite and Web3 wallet. If you want futures, options and stock exposure behind one login, that's Gate's case, and it's a product argument rather than a fee argument.

Take the volume and market-share figures as self-reported. They come from the exchange describing itself, and no independent auditor signs off on them.

## Access and licensing, which overrules every number above

| Japan | Serves residents through Gate Japan K.K., FSA registration 00018 | Not registered; app downloads suspended at the FSA's request in 2025 |

Gate's licence list is the longer of the two: MiCA authorisation from Malta's MFSA, a Dubai VARA operational licence since April 2025, FSA registration in Japan, AUSTRAC registration in Australia, money transmitter licences in 47 US jurisdictions, and a PSD2 payment institution licence from the MFSA in February 2026. Its June 2026 proof-of-reserves disclosed $8.182 billion in total reserves at a 115% coverage ratio across nearly 500 user assets, with BTC at 115.4% and USDC at 118.5%.

KuCoin's regulatory file is the messier one, and it's documented rather than rumoured. Its operating entity pleaded guilty in January 2025 to one count of unlicensed money transmission, with $184.5 million in forfeiture and a $112.9 million fine. It moved its domicile to the Turks and Caicos Islands around September 2025 after Seychelles refused a licence — and the Turks and Caicos regulator then published a statement in November 2025 saying the entity wasn't licensed or authorised to carry out virtual asset business there. Its Austrian MiCA licence was granted on 27 November 2025; Austria's FMA barred it from onboarding new customers from 19 February 2026 over staffing gaps in its AML roles, then lifted that ban on 18 May 2026 while noting that commencement of business operations remained prohibited under a separate decision. A March 2026 CFTC consent order added a $500,000 penalty and a permanent injunction, removing the time limit that the plea deal had put on its US exit.

If you're in the EEA and considering KuCoin, check the Austrian FMA register yourself before you deposit. That's not a hedge, it's just where the authoritative answer lives.

## Security records, both of them

KuCoin's major breach was on the night of 25 to 26 September 2020, not 2021 as it's often reported. Roughly $281 million left its hot wallets, about 84% was recovered through on-chain tracing and freezes at other exchanges, and KuCoin's insurance fund covered the rest, so no customer lost money. It publishes Merkle-tree proof of reserves audited by Mazars.

Gate's own notable incident is more recent and less well documented publicly: a July 2026 dispute over an account theft drew attention, and Gate stated at the time that it would fully compensate losses attributable to its own systems.

Neither exchange has a clean sheet, and anyone telling you otherwise is selling something. One nine-figure breach handled with full repayment eight years ago and one disputed account incident are different kinds of risk, and you should size your on-exchange balance against the one you consider worse rather than the one you consider solved.

## What a new account actually gets

Gate's Rewards Hub runs a welcome package split into tasks — register, complete identity verification, make a first deposit, place a first spot or futures trade — followed by a ladder of deposit and trading challenges. Treat that as a normal exchange promotion: the headline number requires volume you probably aren't planning to generate, the realistic entry-level payout is a fraction of it, rewards generally arrive as trading credits and fee rebate vouchers rather than withdrawable cash, and unlocking them usually requires KYC and hitting volume conditions. ## So which one

There is no universal winner here, and any comparison that declares one is answering a different question than yours.

- Trading majors on spot with bots: KuCoin. Six free native bots and a 0.08% effective rate with KCS are a coherent package.
- Trading small caps and new listings: Gate. Twice the tracked coin count and no 0.30% pair class working against you on every altcoin order.
- Futures and perpetuals: Gate, by about $60 a year per $100,000 of monthly notional at entry tier. Modest, real, and it compounds at size.
- One account for crypto, options and stocks: Gate.
- Living in Japan: Gate, because KuCoin isn't registered there.
- Living in the US or UK: neither. Pick a domestically regulated venue and don't VPN around a block, since that breaches the terms and puts withdrawals at risk.
- Weighting regulatory standing heavily: Gate's licence list and published reserve ratio are the stronger package, and KuCoin's own experience with its domicile and European regulators is the reason that matters.

The honest summary is that the fee question is a dead heat decided by how you trade, and the country question is not close at all.

## FAQ

**Is Gate cheaper than KuCoin?**
On futures, yes, at the entry tier: 0.020%/0.050% against 0.020%/0.060%. On spot, it depends entirely on which KuCoin pair class you're trading — Class A is level at 0.10%, while Class B and C cost two to three times what Gate charges on standard spot pairs.

**Which exchange lists more coins?**
Gate, by a wide margin on CoinGecko's independently tracked figures — roughly 1,550 to 1,690 coins versus 845 to 889 for KuCoin. Both exchanges' own marketing claims run significantly higher than CoinGecko's numbers.

**Can I use either exchange in the US?**
No. KuCoin blocked US-identified accounts in January 2025 and a March 2026 CFTC order made the ban open-ended. Gate's global platform isn't offered to US residents either, though a separate Gate US entity exists with a very limited listing roster.

**Is the 0.20% figure I see for Gate's spot fees correct?**
No. Gate's own fee overview page shows 0.10% maker and taker at VIP 0. The 0.20% number circulating in some comparison tables appears to trace back to an outdated page that other sites copied.

**Can I move assets between the two?**
Yes, by withdrawing from one to your deposit address on the other over a shared network. Send a small test amount first — an address or network mismatch is the most common way people lose funds in a transfer between exchanges, and neither platform will recover it for you.
